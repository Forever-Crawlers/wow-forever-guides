# Orc Hunter: Levels 1–60

This is a complete Horde path from the Valley of Trials to level 60. It follows level-ordered acts with checklists, class lessons, and gear checks. Use it as a learning route, not a mandatory speedrun.

If this is your first Horde character, read the [Horde New Player Guide](/docs/new-player/horde) for installation, interface, travel, professions, and grouping basics. This page focuses on the Orc Hunter journey.

## Evidence and How to Use This Guide

- **Current/confirmed** describes class systems supported by current references or the source document's confirmed design ledger.
- **Beta-reported** identifies quest names, trainer levels, exact tuning, tame tables, vendor locations, and other details that can change.
- **Guide recommendation** marks a route, talent, pet, equipment, profession, or efficiency choice rather than a game rule.

Play the act matching your level. When the current game, quest log, or ability tooltip disagrees with this guide, trust the game and update the guide.

## Act 0: Setup and Meeting Friends

Orc Hunter is available with the normal Forever subscription or Game Time and does not require a separate race unlock. Coordinate **Horde** and the same **Ruleset** with friends before creating characters.

- [ ] Choose **Orc** and **Hunter** at character creation.
- [ ] Install updates before a planned group session.
- [ ] Start in Durotar alongside any Orc or Troll friends rather than traveling elsewhere during the opening levels.
- [ ] Plan an Orgrimmar or Ragefire Chasm meetup when the group reaches an appropriate level.
- [ ] Choose a name you will still enjoy at 60.

**Lesson:** Orc and Troll players can share the opening zone immediately. Other Horde friends can join later using Horde travel connections.

## Act 1: Valley of Trials — Levels 1–4

You begin without a pet. Use ranged attacks while an enemy approaches and finish with melee when it gets close.

- [ ] Accept the opening quests around the Valley hub and complete them in a manageable loop.
- [ ] Open with **Auto Shot** at range, move away when practical, and use **Raptor Strike** when the enemy reaches melee range.
- [ ] Fight one enemy at a time and watch for nearby enemies before attacking.
- [ ] Learn **Serpent Sting** at level 4 and review its damage-over-time effect and Mana cost.
- [ ] Loot useful items, sell unwanted gray items, and keep ammunition stocked.
- [ ] Start Skinning if you want a gathering profession that fits the beasts you already fight.

:::note Beta-reported route and trainer details
The source's exact Valley quest order and Serpent Sting at level 4 come from beta and launch-week observations. Current quests and trainer offers take priority.
:::

**Lesson:** Ranged openings, controlled movement, and single-target pulls form the Hunter's early combat foundation.

- [ ] **Gear check:** Keep your equipped ranged weapon, ammunition, and bag space ready for the next loop.

## Act 2: Early Durotar and Razor Hill — Levels 4–8

Follow the quest road through Durotar toward Razor Hill. Use each settlement as a hub: collect quests, complete a loop, return to turn in, and restock.

- [ ] Complete nearby objectives instead of making separate trips for one drop.
- [ ] Learn **Arcane Shot** at level 6 and **Hunter's Mark** as they become available.
- [ ] Visit vendors for ammunition, recovery supplies, and suitable bag or quiver upgrades.
- [ ] Learn **Concussive Shot** at level 8 and use its slow to control distance.
- [ ] Compare quest rewards with your leather armor and ranged weapon.
- [ ] Start First Aid if you want another recovery tool between or during difficult fights.

:::note Beta-reported route and trainer details
The source's exact Durotar quest order and Arcane Shot/Concussive Shot levels are beta/launch-week observations. Check current trainer offers and quest objectives.
:::

**Lesson:** Auto Shot provides free sustained damage. Spend Mana when it improves a fight, not simply because an ability is available.

- [ ] **Gear check:** Keep ammunition and recovery supplies stocked and your current ranged weapon equipped.

## Act 3: Pet Day — Level 10

At level 10, begin **Taming the Beast** at **Razor Hill**. Complete the class questline before moving on; the class becomes much more comfortable once a permanent pet can hold enemy attention.

- [ ] Follow the questline's practice taming steps with the provided Taming Rod.
- [ ] Complete the follow-up that teaches **Feed Pet** and **Beast Training**.
- [ ] Choose a convenient nearby beast for your first permanent pet rather than chasing a rare ideal companion.
- [ ] Feed your pet suitable food and monitor its happiness.
- [ ] Let the pet attack first and establish threat before spending heavily on shots.
- [ ] Review Mend Pet and pet-support abilities as they become available.
- [ ] Use a stable when you later need to tame a temporary ability carrier without losing your main companion.

**Guide recommendation:** A cat is a common damage-oriented first pet, while a boar is a convenient open-world choice with an easy early route. Pick one you can safely tame and feed.

**Lesson:** Your pet is part of your character. Happiness, health, threat, food, and training all matter.

- [ ] **Gear check:** Carry suitable pet food and ammunition before leaving town.
- [ ] **Solo check:** Growl can help your pet hold threat while questing alone. Turn its automatic use off when joining a dungeon tank.

## Act 4: Finishing Durotar and Orgrimmar — Levels 10–12

Use your new pet to finish the remaining Durotar quest loops and visit Orgrimmar for services.

- [ ] Complete remaining Razor Hill, cultist, harpy, and nearby zone objectives in grouped loops.
- [ ] Visit Orgrimmar for class trainers, weapon masters, bank, auction house, and repairs.
- [ ] Train weapon types you expect to use, including two-handed axes if you want to use the Orc racial association.
- [ ] Raise new weapon skills through ordinary combat rather than waiting until an important drop.
- [ ] Prepare for **Ragefire Chasm** with a group when your level and quests align.
- [ ] Keep Growl off in the dungeon so your pet does not compete with the tank.

:::note Beta-reported route details
Named Durotar quest arcs, Orgrimmar weapon-training choices, and the exact dungeon meetup level come from the source's route data. Current quests and group readiness take priority.
:::

**Lesson:** Orgrimmar is your early service hub. Preparing weapons, supplies, quests, and pet behavior in one visit prevents repeated trips.

- [ ] **Gear check:** Review weapon skills, pet happiness, bags, ammunition, and dungeon quests before the next session.

## Act 5: The Barrens Loop — Levels 12–16

Travel to the Crossroads and use it as your Barrens hub.

- [ ] Take the flight point and set a useful Hearthstone location.
- [ ] Accept several quests that share an area or direction.
- [ ] Complete directional loops and return to turn in, sell, repair, and restock.
- [ ] At level 14, look for the quest leading to the **Cozy Sleeping Bag** and its experience benefit.
- [ ] Use campsites and shared profession benefits when practical.
- [ ] Send your pet toward the next intentional target while you loot the previous enemy.
- [ ] Log out in an inn, city, or other useful rested situation.

**Guide recommendation:** The Crossroads is the default hub for this route because it supports several early quest loops and group connections. Stonetalon is an alternative when you want a different zone or the Barrens is crowded.

**Lesson:** Quest batching, rested preparation, and the pet's kill cadence make the Barrens loop efficient without requiring a speedrun route.

- [ ] **Gear check:** Review your ranged weapon, quiver or ammunition storage, pet food, and current ability ranks after each hub loop.

## Act 6: Level 20 and the Full Toolkit — Levels 18–21

At level 18, review **Multi-Shot**. It provides multi-target damage, but it can also draw additional threat or strike an unintended target. Use it deliberately.

At level 20, visit the trainer and review:

- [ ] **Aimed Shot** for a substantial weapon-damage-based attack.
- [ ] **Aspect of the Cheetah** for travel.
- [ ] **Freezing Trap** for controlling a chosen enemy.
- [ ] **Disengage** for creating distance and managing threat.
- [ ] New ranks of earlier abilities.

Practice control when you are not in danger: place a trap, create distance, use Concussive Shot, and let your pet re-establish threat. In Forever, traps can be used in combat and are placed at your feet; read current shared-cooldown and target rules before using them in a crowded pull.

**Guide recommendation:** Run **Wailing Caverns** once with its quests when a suitable group is available. Continue questing otherwise; the dungeon is optional.

:::note Beta-reported trainer and route details
The exact level-18/20 trainer milestones and Wailing Caverns route come from beta/launch-week data. Current trainers, tooltips, and dungeon quests take priority.
:::

**Lesson:** Hunters solve many problems with distance and control rather than interrupts or a dedicated tank role.

- [ ] **Gear check:** Favor a suitable high-damage ranged weapon and keep your pet healthy, happy, and current on abilities.

## Act 7: The 20s — Levels 21–30

Beast Mastery, pet progression, and wider Horde routes define this band.

- [ ] Continue Beast Mastery if you want the guide's pet-centered leveling recommendation.
- [ ] Choose early talents based on the problem you have: pet durability, pet damage, attack speed, movement, or pet recovery.
- [ ] Take **Summon Hawk** when your current build reaches its Beast Mastery milestone and review how it fits your ability use.
- [ ] Temporarily tame beasts to learn higher ability ranks, then teach eligible ranks to your main pet through Beast Training.
- [ ] Use southern Barrens, Stonetalon, Hillsbrad, or other suitable zones for your level and group.
- [ ] At level 30, train **Feign Death** and use it as a combat reset when a fight becomes unsafe.
- [ ] In dungeons, keep Growl off and let the tank control threat.

:::note Beta-reported talent and tame details
The source estimates Summon Hawk at about level 25 and lists specific pet locations and ability ranks. These details can move; current talents, Beast Training, and wild-pet tooltips take priority.
:::

**Guide recommendation:** Pet durability and pet damage are both reasonable early choices. Choose durability if your pet keeps dying and damage when it is already stable.

**Lesson:** Pet management is an ongoing system. Happiness, ability ranks, stables, talents, and gear scaling all contribute to the character.

- [ ] **Gear check:** Review ranged weapon upgrades and pet ability ranks as you enter new zones.

## Act 8: The 30s — Levels 30–39

Use Arathi Highlands, the Shimmering Flats, Desolace, or other appropriate Horde zones. You have more viable routes than you need; choose by level, quests, and group availability.

- [ ] Take flight points at hubs such as Hammerfall and Freewind Post.
- [ ] Continue Beast Mastery toward **Bestial Wrath** and review its pet-enhancing effect when unlocked.
- [ ] Practice recovering from a failed fight with Feign Death, healing, and Revive Pet.
- [ ] Learn higher pet ability ranks from suitable wild beasts as you travel.
- [ ] Keep a current ranged weapon and useful Agility equipment.
- [ ] Save for level-40 training and mount expenses.

Optional dungeons in this broader band require route judgment:

- [ ] Consider **Excavation Site: Wetlands** only with a prepared group and a quest reason for the Alliance-adjacent trip.
- [ ] Approach **City of Dalaran** only with a current Horde access plan and prepared group.

:::note Beta-reported tame and route details
Specific bears, wolves, crocolisks, and other ability carriers in the source's tame table are beta observations. Use Beast Training and current beast tooltips to verify available ranks.
:::

**Lesson:** Weapon upgrades, pet ability progression, and recovery practice keep the leveling machine running across multiple zones.

- [ ] **Gear check:** Maintain weapon damage, Agility, ammunition, and pet ability ranks rather than buying every armor upgrade.

## Act 9: Riverglades and the Mount — Levels 40–45

At level 40, review mail armor training and your current Orc mount/riding options. The Hunter route does not provide the Paladin's class-mount exemption, so plan around current prices.

- [ ] Train and equip suitable mail armor.
- [ ] Purchase or use a mount you can afford, checking the current vendor and riding requirements.
- [ ] Enter **Riverglades** during its appropriate mid-level band and follow the main quest arc.
- [ ] Prepare for **Krol'dok Stronghold** with its quests when your group reaches the dungeon band.
- [ ] Use **The Drowned City** as an optional 35–40 alternative when its level and quests fit.
- [ ] Review **Aspect of the Pack** carefully before using a group movement effect around enemies.
- [ ] Continue Bestial Wrath and pet-centered combat as your build develops.

:::note Beta-reported route and mount details
The source's Drowned City band, Orc wolf vendor location, and gold estimates are route/beta details. Check current dungeon availability, mount vendor, riding requirements, and price instead of relying on specific estimates.
:::

**Lesson:** Mail, a mount, and a mature pet build make the 40s smoother. Riverglades provides a structured zone and dungeon chapter.

- [ ] **Gear check:** Use suitable Agility mail, a current ranged weapon, stocked ammunition, and a well-trained pet.

## Act 10: The Road to 60 — Levels 45–60

Use later zones and selected dungeons to finish the journey. The source route offers Felwood, Tanaris, the Hinterlands, Azshara, Un'Goro, the Blasted Lands, and Winterspring across different bands.

- [ ] Choose zones appropriate to your current level rather than following a route after its quests have become trivial.
- [ ] Consider **Alcaz Prison**, **Blackmaw Hold**, and **Shaper's Terrace** at their appropriate dungeon bands.
- [ ] Collect dungeon quests and prepare role-appropriate equipment before each run.
- [ ] Continue learning higher pet ability ranks and teaching them to your main companion.
- [ ] Spend later talent points according to your chosen damage, durability, range, or utility needs.
- [ ] At 60, choose Beast Mastery, Marksmanship, or Survival based on how you want to play.
- [ ] Review **Tranquilizing Shot** and other utility expected by current group content.
- [ ] Check current raid schedules and preparation. The source lists Barrow Deeps, Hyjal Summit, and Onyxia's Lair as scheduled for December 9, 2026.

**Guide recommendation:** Marksmanship's Lone Wolf offers a petless direction, but do not abandon a pet-based rotation without learning the current talent and gameplay requirements. Survival offers another identity; choose based on experience rather than a label.

:::note Beta-reported route details
Exact late-zone order, dungeon sequencing, pet carriers, and endgame tuning retain the source's beta route and recommendations. Current availability and tooltips take priority.
:::

**Lesson:** Reach 60 with a current weapon, maintained pet or deliberate petless build, useful utility, and a group plan.

- [ ] **Gear check:** Equip the best suitable ranged weapon available and stock the correct ammunition for endgame preparation.
- [ ] **Epic-riding handoff:** Begin building your **epic-riding fund** using current riding prices rather than an estimated cost.

## Level-60 Character Audit

- [ ] Choose a specialization with a coherent talent plan.
- [ ] Maintain your chosen pet's level, happiness, and ability ranks, or understand a deliberate petless setup.
- [ ] Keep useful stable options and room for temporary ability-learning tames.
- [ ] Equip the best suitable ranged weapon and stock the correct ammunition.
- [ ] Train and raise the weapon skills you expect to use.
- [ ] Keybind and practice traps, Concussive Shot, Intimidation, Disengage, and Feign Death as available.
- [ ] Understand when Growl belongs on or off.
- [ ] Use combat and travel Aspects deliberately, including the risks of Pack.
- [ ] Maintain professions, supplies, and recovery tools that support your play.
- [ ] Find a guild or steady group whose expectations fit you.

## Hunter Leveling Systems

### Pets

Pets scale with your gear in Forever, so upgrading your own stats also strengthens your companion. Pet families have distinct abilities, while Ferocity, Cunning, and Tenacity classifications do not create a simple baseline damage winner.

Feed your pet and monitor happiness and loyalty. Learn higher ability ranks by temporarily taming beasts that know them, then teach eligible ranks to your main pet. Use Growl to help your pet tank alone and turn it off for a dungeon tank. Mend Pet and Revive Pet keep the companion system operational.

### Shots and Mana

Auto Shot and your pet provide substantial damage without constant spending. Use Hunter's Mark, Serpent Sting, Aimed Shot, Arcane Shot, Multi-Shot, and Summon Hawk according to their current costs, cooldowns, targets, and interactions rather than pressing everything on every enemy.

Ranged attacks have distance limitations. When an enemy gets too close, use melee or movement tools to restore a useful range instead of repeatedly attempting invalid ranged attacks.

### Aspects

- **Hawk:** Combat-oriented choice.
- **Cheetah:** Personal travel speed.
- **Monkey:** Defensive option.
- **Pack:** Group movement utility that requires caution around enemies.

Read current tooltips and switch deliberately rather than leaving a travel or group Aspect active in the wrong situation.

### Traps in Combat

Traps can be used in combat and placed at your feet in Forever. Frost Trap supports area slowing and escapes; Freezing Trap controls a chosen enemy; damage traps serve offensive situations. Current shared-cooldown rules make placement a decision rather than an unconditional rotation.

### Feign Death, Disengage, and Threat

Feign Death at 30 provides a combat reset when not resisted. Disengage at 20 creates distance and manages threat. Neither replaces paying attention to your pet, the tank, or nearby enemies.

### Orc Racials

**Command is removed in Forever.** Do not choose Orc based on the old pet-damage bonus.

The current Orc package includes Hardiness, Shatter Curse, Blood Fury, and Axe Specialization. These offer stun, curse/magic-defense, offensive cooldown, and axe-related benefits rather than a permanent Hunter pet bonus. Read current tooltips for exact values.

### Professions and Money

**Guide recommendations:** Skinning and Leatherworking fit a beast-heavy Hunter route; Engineering and Mining offer gadgets and crafting; gathering professions support income. Cooking, Fishing, and First Aid provide additional supplies and campsite participation.

Hunters do not receive the Paladin's class-mount route. Save for current mount and riding costs, and prioritize useful weapons, bags, ammunition, and pet supplies over every minor armor purchase.

### Dungeon Role

Hunters join as damage dealers with utility: Hunter's Mark, Tranquilizing Shot, traps, pulling tools, pet control, and threat management. Follow the tank, watch the healer's Mana, keep Growl off, and ask before Need rolls.

## Speed and Efficiency Rules

- [ ] Kill enemies sequentially rather than expecting a pet to hold an uncontrolled pack.
- [ ] Let Auto Shot and your pet work while managing Mana.
- [ ] Prepare dungeon quests before entering; repeated farming is a separate choice.
- [ ] Use rested areas, campsites, and shared profession benefits.
- [ ] Pick up the Cozy Sleeping Bag when its quest becomes available.
- [ ] Keep ammunition, pet food, and happiness ready before long loops.
- [ ] Let your pet establish threat before attacking.
- [ ] Use Feign Death when a fight turns instead of treating it as a last-act rescue.
- [ ] Train weapon skills before you need an unfamiliar drop.
- [ ] Review weapon and pet ability upgrades as you enter new level bands.
