# Orc Hunter: Levels 1–20

This guide takes a new Orc Hunter from the Valley of Trials to level 20. It is a learning path, not a speedrun. Follow the level bands in order, take detours when something interests you, and use the checklists to prepare for the next area.

If this is your first Horde character, read the [Horde New Player Guide](/docs/new-player/horde) for installation, interface, travel, and grouping basics. This page focuses on what changes when you play a Hunter.

## What to Expect

Hunters fight at range with bows, guns, ammunition, and a mix of class abilities. In Forever, Hunters use **Mana** as their class resource.

Your first ten levels teach you to fight without a permanent pet. At level 10, the **Taming the Beast** class questline changes the class completely: your pet can hold an enemy's attention while you attack from safety.

By level 20, you should understand how to:

- Pull one enemy at a time and let Auto Shot do steady damage.
- Manage Mana instead of spending every ability immediately.
- Feed and protect your pet.
- Keep ammunition and a suitable ranged weapon equipped.
- Prepare for an optional dungeon without disrupting the group.

Beast Mastery is a forgiving talent direction for this journey because it strengthens the pet who makes solo combat comfortable. You do not need a perfect point-by-point build to learn the class.

## Core Hunter Habits

These habits matter more than optimizing a route:

1. **Look before you pull.** Check for nearby enemies and patrols. One intentional enemy is easier than three accidental ones.
2. **Start at range.** Use Auto Shot and your learned abilities while the enemy approaches. If it reaches melee range, continue with melee attacks and Raptor Strike rather than trying to run in circles forever.
3. **Let passive damage work.** Auto Shot and, later, your pet provide substantial damage. Spending all your Mana on every fight forces more recovery time.
4. **Loot, sell, repair, restock.** Visit vendors before bags break a questing session. Keep ammunition appropriate to your bow or gun.
5. **Keep your ranged weapon current.** Weapon damage is especially important to Hunters. Compare weapon damage and useful stats rather than looking only at item color.
6. **Wear equipment that helps you.** Early Hunters use leather armor. Agility is a useful damage stat; Stamina and other stats can still help you survive or recover.

## Levels 1–4: Valley of Trials

You begin without a pet. Your job is to learn the rhythm of ranged opening attacks followed by melee when an enemy gets close.

### Your first loop

- Accept the introductory quests around the Den and read their objectives.
- Fight nearby boars and other quest enemies one at a time.
- Begin at range with Auto Shot. Use your available abilities, then finish enemies that reach you with melee attacks and Raptor Strike.
- Loot defeated enemies and sell unwanted gray items when you return.
- Keep your ammunition stocked. A Hunter without the correct arrows or bullets cannot use their ranged attacks normally.
- Visit the class trainer when you reach level 4 and learn **Serpent Sting**.

Serpent Sting adds damage over time. It is useful, but you do not need to apply it to every enemy if the target will fall quickly or your Mana needs a rest.

### Before moving on

- [ ] You can target an enemy and begin an attack at range.
- [ ] You have completed and turned in your first quests.
- [ ] You have sold unwanted items and checked your ammunition.
- [ ] Serpent Sting is on your action bar.

## Levels 5–7: Early Durotar and Sen'jin Village

Follow the early quest path through Durotar and toward Sen'jin Village. Treat each settlement as a hub: collect quests, complete a manageable loop, return to turn them in, then prepare for the next loop.

At level 6, visit a trainer for **Arcane Shot** and **Hunter's Mark**. Hunter's Mark helps you identify and pressure a chosen target; Arcane Shot gives you another ranged option. Read their current tooltips as you add them to your action bar.

### Hub routine

1. Accept quests you intend to complete in the same general area.
2. Set your Hearthstone at a useful inn and locate vendors.
3. Complete nearby objectives without constantly crossing the zone for a single drop.
4. Return, turn in quests, sell unwanted items, repair when needed, and restock.
5. Compare quest rewards with your equipped leather armor and ranged weapon.

If a quest sends you far away from everything else you are doing, leave it for a later trip. You do not need to accept every long detour immediately.

## Levels 8–10: Razor Hill

Continue toward Razor Hill and use it as your next base. Take the available flight point when you reach it so later travel is easier.

At level 8, train **Concussive Shot**. Its slowing effect gives you more control over an approaching enemy. It is particularly useful when you need to keep distance or discourage an enemy from escaping into another pack.

### Preparing for level 10

- Keep enough ammunition for several quest loops.
- Review your ranged weapon and replace it when a suitable upgrade appears.
- Learn new ability ranks when a trainer visit is already convenient.
- Read the Beast Mastery talents as they become available. Talents begin contributing to your build during this stretch; you do not need to respec after every choice.
- Finish nearby quest work before beginning the class quest at level 10.

Do not worry about finding a theoretically perfect early talent order. Learn what your choices do and favor Beast Mastery if you want the most pet-centered experience.

## Level 10: Your First Pet

At level 10, begin the **Taming the Beast** class questline. For Orc and Troll Hunters, the current Forever leveling references place this quest in **Razor Hill**.

The questline teaches temporary taming with a Taming Rod before you permanently adopt a pet. Follow each step rather than simply trying to tame a random beast immediately. The later capital-city follow-up teaches important pet abilities, including **Feed Pet** and **Beast Training**.

### Choose a convenient first companion

Pick a nearby beast that you can safely tame and feed. A boar is a convenient open-world companion; a cat is another common choice. Your first pet does not need to be rare or perfect.

Before taming:

- Clear or avoid nearby hostile enemies so they cannot interrupt you.
- Review the target's level and your current abilities.
- Make sure you have a safe place to stand for the taming process.

After taming:

- Feed your pet with suitable food and watch its happiness.
- Let the pet attack first and establish threat before you spend heavily on shots.
- Use Feed Pet outside the heat of combat when needed.
- Open Beast Training and review what your pet can learn.

Pet ability ranks can be learned by temporarily taming beasts that already know those abilities and then teaching the learned rank to an eligible companion. You can explore that system gradually; your first goal is simply keeping one pet healthy and useful.

:::warning Your pet is not extra baggage
A hungry or unhappy pet is weaker and may leave you. Check its happiness before a long quest loop, not after it becomes a problem.
:::

## Levels 10–12: The Crossroads

Travel west into the Barrens and make your way to the Crossroads. Use the roads and watch enemy levels along the way.

When you arrive:

1. Take the flight point.
2. Set your Hearthstone if this will be your current quest hub.
3. Locate ammunition, food, repair, and other useful vendors.
4. Accept a manageable group of nearby quests.
5. Restock pet food before leaving town.

### Your new combat cadence

Your pet now leads the fight:

1. Send the pet toward one target.
2. Give it a moment to establish threat.
3. Begin Auto Shot and use abilities as Mana allows.
4. Watch both your health and your pet's health.
5. After the kill, send your pet toward the next intentional target while you loot.

Do not attack several enemies simply because your pet can fight more than one. Pets have limited ability to hold groups together, especially when you add area damage. Sequential kills are safer and usually smoother.

Review pet support abilities such as Mend Pet as they become available through your current class and pet training. A healthy pet is your main defensive tool.

## Levels 13–16: Early Barrens and Optional Ragefire Chasm

The Crossroads offers enough quests to practice full hub loops. Work by direction rather than running back and forth across the zone: finish a group of nearby objectives, return to town, and cash out the loop.

At level 14, watch for the quest leading to the **Cozy Sleeping Bag**. Current Forever leveling guides recommend picking up this experience-boosting item when it becomes available. Treat it as a worthwhile side objective, not something that should block your normal questing.

### Optional first dungeon: Ragefire Chasm

At level 13 or later, you can consider **Ragefire Chasm** in Orgrimmar. It is optional, but a first dungeon is a useful way to learn group combat.

Before entering:

- Form a five-player group with a tank, a healer, and damage dealers.
- Collect the available dungeon quests before starting the run.
- Repair, restock ammunition and supplies, and make room in your bags.
- Turn off your pet's automatic **Growl**. Your pet should not compete with the tank for control of dungeon enemies.

Inside, let the tank lead and attack the target the group is using. Do not send your pet into a separate pack. If you are unsure whether to roll Need on an item, ask first.

One quest-prepared run is a useful milestone. You do not need to repeatedly clear the dungeon just to gain levels.

## Levels 17–20: Wailing Caverns and New Tools

Continue using the Barrens hubs and quest loops. At level 18, train **Multi-Shot**. It gives you a multi-target option, but more targets also mean more threat and more chances to disturb an unintended pack. Use it deliberately rather than automatically.

At this stage, **Wailing Caverns** becomes an optional dungeon adventure in the northwest Barrens. Prepare by collecting its quests and applying the same rules learned in Ragefire Chasm: tank leads, pet Growl off, attack the group's target, and ask about loot.

You can continue questing if a group is not available. Dungeons are valuable for quests, equipment, and learning group play, but they are not a requirement for following this guide.

### Level 20 unlocks

At level 20, visit the trainer and review:

- **Aspect of the Cheetah** for faster travel.
- **Aimed Shot** for another ranged attack option.
- **Freezing Trap** for controlling a chosen enemy.
- Available upgrades to earlier abilities.

Aspect of the Cheetah is a travel tool. Read its tooltip and use it thoughtfully rather than leaving every movement buff active without considering the situation. Freezing Trap can remove one enemy from a fight temporarily; avoid damaging the trapped target until the group is ready.

You now have the basic Hunter toolkit: ranged pressure, a pet, mobility, and control.

## Gear, Talents, and Professions

### Gear

Prioritize a suitable ranged weapon and useful leather armor. Weapon damage and Agility are reliable things to understand first. Keep the correct ammunition equipped and stocked, and use a quiver or ammo pouch when appropriate.

Do not buy every armor upgrade offered by a vendor. Quest rewards and occasional dungeon upgrades can carry you while you save for supplies and future expenses.

### Talents

Beast Mastery remains the beginner-friendly direction because it improves the pet-centered combat you have been practicing. Current Forever guides differ on the most efficient early point order because damage and pet-survivability choices solve different problems. Read the options and choose the one addressing your actual difficulty; there is no need to copy a speedrunning build for your first character.

### Professions

**Skinning and Leatherworking** are a natural Hunter pair: beasts you defeat provide materials, and Leatherworking can turn those materials into useful equipment. They are a recommendation, not a requirement.

Cooking, Fishing, and First Aid can all support your journey. Visit campsites, share any profession benefits you can provide, and use the shared preparation before a difficult trip.

## Level-20 Checklist

Before moving beyond this guide, check that:

- [ ] Your current ranged weapon and leather armor are suitable for your level.
- [ ] You carry the correct ammunition and basic recovery supplies.
- [ ] Your pet is fed, happy, and using the abilities you expect.
- [ ] You can let your pet establish threat before attacking.
- [ ] You have visited a trainer and reviewed your level-20 abilities.
- [ ] You have useful flight points and a sensible Hearthstone location.
- [ ] You know to disable automatic Growl in a dungeon.
- [ ] You can ask a group before making a questionable Need roll.

If those habits are in place, you are ready for the next level band.
