# Troll Warrior: Levels 1–60

Normal ruleset — Horde — Troll Warrior. From first login in the Valley of Trials to the level-60 ceiling. Same skeleton as the other class guides: acts in level order, a checkbox per step, a short lesson after each act, and the exception tables at the end.

Two things about this combination before starting. First, troll is widely rated the best leveling race for Warriors in Forever — launch-week class guides say so openly. Second, Warrior has the hardest first ten levels of any class and the strongest payoff curve after. This guide is honest about both.

## Act 0 — Launch Night Setup

No pack, no unlock. Troll Warrior is default character creation. The checklist is about the hardest leveling hours in the game, which are the ones directly ahead.

### Before Logging In

- [ ] Confirm the subscription is active.
- [ ] Pick the server with the group. Troll is Horde; faction is decided.
- [ ] Install updates and launch once before the session that matters.

### Character Creation

- [ ] Race: Troll. Class: Warrior.
- [ ] Name the character. It will be said for 150 hours.

On racials: Berserking is 15% attack speed for 10 seconds on a 2-minute cooldown, and for a Warrior attack speed is rage generation — Berserking is a resource button pressed every fight. Beast Slaying adds 5% damage against beasts, which describes most of what gets killed until 60. Regeneration is passive health regen, and it multiplies with Forever's Spirit scaling for Warriors. Launch-week class guides call troll the best leveling race for the class. They are right.

### Meetup Plan With Friends

Trolls and orcs share the Valley of Trials. A friend who rolled orc hunter starts in the same camp from level 1:

- [ ] Levels 1–12: quest together in Durotar. Shared kill quests, shared campfire buffs, zero travel.
- [ ] The pair is nasty: the pet tanks while rage builds, and Berserking plus Serpent Sting deletes elites the pair has no business killing.
- [ ] Around 13: group for Ragefire Chasm together. The Warrior tanks it in Defensive Stance; the hunter loots.
- [ ] Splitting for solo sessions is fine, but the duo is faster than either class alone until at least 30.

**What this act teaches:** the best leveling race for the class, a shared start with a hunter partner, and the honest warning that Warrior's worst hours are its first ten.

## Act 1 — The Valley of Trials, Levels 1–4

Warrior has the weakest opening of any class: almost no rage, almost no buttons, every fight starting from zero. Forever softens it with normalized rage and Victory Rush, but the first four levels still teach one lesson: pick fights that can be won, one at a time, always.

**Goal:** level 4, core buttons learned, First Aid started.

- [ ] Take every quest in the Valley hub and sweep the canyon loop. One enemy at a time, always. Warriors who pull two at level 3 are dead warriors.
- [ ] Learn Heroic Strike at the trainer. It converts the next auto-attack into a real hit; press it only when rage allows — it is a rage dump, not a rotation.
- [ ] Learn Rend at 4. The most rage-efficient damage available for the next 20 levels. Put it on everything that will live 9 seconds or more. Rends do not stack: a second cast refreshes the bleed and discards the remaining ticks, so apply it once and let it run. Separate warriors each maintain their own Rend, and Deep Wounds from crits coexists with it — only same-caster reapplication overwrites.
- [ ] Learn Charge at 4. The opener, the gap closer, the escape-rage generator. Always open with it. It generates 9 Rage (rank 1) and stuns for 1 second; it has a 15 sec cooldown and requires being out of combat.
- [ ] Expect the first Rend press after a Charge to wait briefly — two gates, not a bug: the 1.5 sec global cooldown that every instant spell triggers, and a rage gap at these levels (Charge grants 9, Rend costs 10), so the first bleed press also waits for the first auto-attack swing to land. Improved Charge 2/2 (+6 Rage, taken at 24–25) closes the rage gap permanently.
- [ ] Start First Aid immediately. Linen drops from the humanoids already being killed. Bandages are the only heal until Victory Rush arrives, and they stay the main heal for 40 levels.
- [ ] Skip Skinning; beasts give a Warrior nothing needed except food. Take Mining if a gathering skill appeals.

**Gear check:** whatever weapon hits hardest — weapon damage is the damage. Any 4–6 slot bags from quests or drops.

**What this act teaches:** rage rules everything. Fights start empty, so the first seconds are weak and the last seconds are strong. Pool rage before hard fights by killing the weak mob next to the strong one. This habit, learned at level 3, is the entire Warrior skill ceiling.

## Act 2 — The Road to Razor Hill, Levels 4–7

The quest line pushes out of the canyon toward Razor Hill, with Sen'jin Village on the coast as the troll-flavored side hub. The buttons arrive: Battle Shout early, Thunder Clap at 6, and the rage economy starts feeling like a class instead of a trickle.

**Goal:** level 7, Battle Shout always up, Thunder Clap learned, kill rhythm stable.

- [ ] Learn Battle Shout at the trainer. Keep it up at all times — the first and cheapest damage buff.
- [ ] Learn Thunder Clap at 6. Damage plus an attack-power debuff on everything nearby. Use it when a fight involves two enemies or one hard one; it is also the best rage-efficient damage per cast at this level.
- [ ] Run the road quests between the Valley and Razor Hill. Clear quilboar camps one mob at a time; they link and they call friends.
- [ ] Do the Sen'jin Village circuit on the coast if the levels fit; the murloc and raptor quests there feed the Barrens breadcrumb later.
- [ ] Use food. Eat to full between pulls; the health bar IS the resource pool, and every point a fight starts with is rage that did not need generating.
- [ ] Keep First Aid leveling on looted linen. Bandage after every second fight; it is faster than eating.

**Gear check:** best 2H weapon from quests or drops — slow is fine now, rage is normalized, so weapon damage per swing is what is being bought. Spirit on gear is secretly the best stat while leveling.

**What this act teaches:** uptime is the Warrior stat. Eating and bandaging between pulls is not downtime, it is how the class plays. The Warriors who hit 60 first are the ones who never fought at half health, not the ones who fought the most.

## Act 3 — Level 10: The Stance Quest and the Tank Switch, Levels 10–11

Level 10 is the Warrior's first fork. The class quest chain in Razor Hill grants Defensive Stance and the tanking toolkit: Taunt, Sunder Armor, and the ability to be the tank for any dungeon. It is where the class stops being a worse rogue and starts being a Warrior.

**Goal:** level 11, Defensive Stance earned, dungeon tanking unlocked.

- [ ] Pick up the Warrior class quest at the Razor Hill trainer and finish the whole chain, ending at the fighting pit quest south of Razor Hill (guide estimate: the blood elf challenger). Every step teaches a mechanic used for 50 levels.
- [ ] Learn Defensive Stance, Taunt, and Sunder Armor from the chain. Sunder five times on any dungeon boss: the armor shred is a party-wide physical damage buff.
- [ ] Practice the stance dance: Battle Stance to fight, Defensive Stance when tanking or when survival matters. Stances sit on their own bar; put the used ones where fingers live.
- [ ] Talent point one at 10 goes to Improved Rend 1/3. The Arms highway starts humbly; the destination is Mortal Strike.
- [ ] Group with a hunter partner for Ragefire Chasm the moment both are ready. The Warrior tanks: Taunt the loose mob, Sunder the skull, let the pet help. Tanked RFC groups clear twice as fast as scared ones.

**Gear check:** a shield on hand for dungeons, even if questing in 2H. Cloth stacks for bandages: 3+ stacks of linen banked.

**What this act teaches:** Defensive Stance makes the Warrior the person dungeons are built around. Warriors tank every Horde 5-man in Forever, and tank groups form instantly. Learning to hold threat at level 10 is learning the endgame job for free.

## Act 4 — Finishing Durotar, Levels 11–13

The last Durotar band runs the zone's edges: Skull Rock's cultists, the harpy cliffs, the last quilboar pushes. With stances, talents, and a partner beside, this is where the class starts proving the best-leveling-race pick was not a marketing line.

**Goal:** level 13, Durotar cleared, Orgrimmar learned, RFC done.

- [ ] Run Skull Rock and the remaining Razor Hill-side quests. Pull cultists singly; they cast and they link.
- [ ] Learn Thunder Clap's rank-ups and keep Demoralizing Shout in mind for when it arrives; reducing what enemies hit for is stronger than hitting them harder.
- [ ] Ride into Orgrimmar. Learn the map: weapon masters (trolls lack a weapon racial — train everything: swords, axes, maces, polearms, staves), the auction house, the bank, and the zeppelin tower south of the gates.
- [ ] RFC with the group: the Warrior tanks, every quest first, one clean clear. Forever's dungeon XP lives in the quests.
- [ ] Train every weapon skill offered in Orgrimmar. Weapon skill is damage, and untrained weapon types are misses that cannot be afforded.

**Gear check:** 2H weapon for questing, shield plus 1H for dungeons, both weapon skills rising. Bags from the auction house: 8+ slots, funded by cloth and grey sales.

**What this act teaches:** Orgrimmar is the service city for the whole run, and weapon skills are the subscription paid to damage. As the tank, the pace of every group is now set here. Act like it: pull, hold, move.

## Act 5 — The Barrens Loop, Levels 13–16

The Barrens is the Horde's leveling basin, and Crossroads is its heart. For a Warrior it is also the campfire economy's home turf: the class benefits from more of the new Forever systems than any other, and this act turns them all on.

**Goal:** level 16, Crossroads hearth set, campfire buffs in rotation.

- [ ] Take the flight path at Crossroads before anything else, then set the hearthstone. Home through the teens.
- [ ] Stack four or five quests per field trip. The Barrens meta is never riding out for one quest.
- [ ] At 14, chase the quest that rewards the **Cozy Sleeping Bag**. The stacking XP buff is worth a detour.
- [ ] Adopt campfire culture. The Basic Campfire Kit comes from Cooking; then add a fixture: Leatherworking's Camp Tent is the best XP-per-hour option, Skinning's Camp Chair gives crit, Blacksmithing's Sharpening Wheel suits Warriors directly. Sit, buff, move. Contribute a profession item to fires passed.
- [ ] Eat Well Fed food at all times. The 5% kill XP from cooked food is the strongest leveling consumable in Forever, and the cheapest food on the auction house is nearly free.
- [ ] Keep First Aid current and use bandages aggressively between pulls. Victory Rush plus bandages means almost never eating mid-session.

**Gear check:** sharpening stone on the weapon at all times — cheap, significant, always on. Spirit and Strength on pieces; Agility is fine; Stamina is lower priority than it looks while solo.

**What this act teaches:** Warrior uptime in Forever is a system stack: Victory Rush heals, Spirit regenerates, bandages patch, campfire buffs amplify, Well Fed multiplies XP. The class with no heal spell became the class with the best sustain economy, and the Barrens is where it gets learned.

## Act 6 — Levels 16–20: The Toolkit Completes

The late teens give the Warrior the full solo kit: Demoralizing Shout, Hamstring, and the rage to use them. Forever's new talents start mattering too, and Deep Wounds turns crits into real damage.

**Goal:** level 20, deep into the Arms tree, dungeon-tank confident.

- [ ] Learn Demoralizing Shout when it arrives at the trainer. Against melee mobs it is the strongest button owned: less damage taken is faster leveling than more damage dealt. Skip it against casters.
- [ ] Learn Hamstring and use it on anything that runs. Mobs fleeing to their friends is how Warriors die.
- [ ] Talents: Improved Rend 3/3 by 12, Deflection through 14, Improved Overpower 2/2 at 15–16 (Overpower after a dodge is the most rage-efficient attack), Improved Charge 2/2, then Deflection 5/5 and Deep Wounds 3/3 by 24. The tree is a highway; stay on it.
- [ ] Run Wailing Caverns once, quest-loaded, as the tank. Taunt the loose, Sunder the boss, Demoralizing Shout every pack.
- [ ] Learn Piercing Howl at 22 off the highway if packs keep escaping; otherwise keep the Arms line pure.

**Gear check:** weapon upgrade around 18 — slow 2H, highest damage per swing affordable. The Arms spec is the most weapon-hungry in the game. Shield updated for dungeons.

**What this act teaches:** the rotation takes shape: Charge in, Battle Shout up, Demoralizing Shout on melee packs, Rend on anything living 9 seconds, Victory Rush under 90% health, Overpower on every dodge, Heroic Strike only to dump excess rage. Every ability earns its rage or it does not get pressed.

## Act 7 — The 20s: Sweeping Strikes and the Wide World, Levels 21–30

The 20s are the Warrior's coming-of-age: Berserker Stance at 30, Intercept, Whirlwind from the talent line, and Sweeping Strikes as the mid-tree crown. In a pair with a hunter, this is the decade where the tank who needed help becomes the reason the pair wins.

**Goal:** level 30, Sweeping Strikes taken, Berserker Stance earned, the Whirlwind Axe decision made.

- [ ] At 25, take Spearing Strike off the Arms highway. From launch-week data it hits hardest against dragonkins and giants; learn which camps qualify and route through them.
- [ ] At 26–28, Two-Handed Weapon Specialization 3/3. At 29–30, Impale 2/2, then Sweeping Strikes at 30. Two-target damage transforms pull options: Sweeping Strikes turns every Cleave into a two-for-one.
- [ ] Do the Berserker Stance quest at 30. Berserker Rage, Intercept, and Pummel come with it: the interrupt (finally), the second gap closer, and the rage burst tool.
- [ ] Decide on the Whirlwind Axe. The level-30 Warrior weapon quest is obtainable at 30 but brutally hard at level on a fresh server; if a partner will help and it can be finished near 33–35, the axe carries to 40. If solo, skip it without regret and buy the auction-house equivalent around 30–32.
- [ ] Run the 20–25 band in southern Barrens, Stonetalon, and Hillsbrad. Tarren Mill is the Hillsbrad hub. Keep the hunter close: the pet holds the second mob while Sweeping Strikes deletes both.

**Gear check:** Whirlwind Axe or auction equivalent. Once the Weaponmaster talent is online, swords beat axes beat maces and staves, and slower is always better for Arms. Auction checks: cheap healing potions, strength elixirs, 8–10 slot bags if still short.

**What this act teaches:** Sweeping Strikes plus Cleave is the duo superpower — two enemies die at once, and pairs of mobs are where other classes slow down. The level-30 stance quest finally gives Warriors an interrupt; before Pummel, casters were kryptonite. Now line-of-sight them around corners or eat the bolts.

## Act 8 — The 30s, Levels 30–39

The 30s reward the prepared: Arms tools keep landing (Weaponmaster, Improved Slam, Bloodthrill), and the zones widen to Arathi, Shimmering Flats, and Desolace. Forever's mobs gain health past 30, so kill speed starts depending on weapon quality more than ever.

**Goal:** level 39 with Mortal Strike queued at 40, a mount fund ready, every weapon skill trained.

- [ ] Continue Arms: Sweeping Strikes at 30, Bloodthrill 2/2, Impale finished, Weaponmaster through 39, Improved Slam at 35 and 41. Slam with Improved Slam is a real filler button; without the talent, never cast it.
- [ ] Execute arrived at 24. Use it only to finish, ideally at 15 rage; dumping 40 rage into it is a damage and threat trap.
- [ ] Run the 30–35 band in Arathi Highlands (Hammerfall), Shimmering Flats, or Desolace. Three zones, pick two by crowd level. Flight paths first, always.
- [ ] Check the Excavation Site dungeon (24–29 band) only if passing the Wetlands with a loaded group; Alliance-adjacent, skippable.
- [ ] City of Dalaran (28–33) is reachable but the guards hate the Horde. Group, summons, one quest-loaded clear, leave.
- [ ] Bank relentlessly. The raptor at 40 is the biggest purchase of the run. Trolls buy theirs in Sen'jin Village — plan the trip.

**Gear check:** auction or quest 2H in the early 30s and again near 38. Spirit pieces are still leveling gold. Stamina matters more in dungeons; swap mentality when queuing as tank.

**What this act teaches:** by the late 30s the two-target engine is fully built: Sweeping Strikes, Cleave, Whirlwind in Berserker Stance for packs, Overpower fishing on dodges. Forever mobs scale past 30, so weapon upgrades stop being suggestions. Weapon first, always.

## Act 9 — Riverglades and the Raptor, Levels 40–45

Two milestones: Mortal Strike at 40, the Arms capstone and the burst identity, and Riverglades, Forever's shared mid-band zone with Krol'dok Stronghold inside its storyline. Plus the mount, paid for with everything banked.

**Goal:** level 45, mounted, Mortal Strike in the bar, Krol'dok cleared once.

- [ ] At 40: Mortal Strike, then the second Improved Slam point. The burst rotation now includes the biggest single button in the leveling tree.
- [ ] Buy the raptor. Troll mounts sell in Sen'jin Village (guide estimate, following classic vendor placement); the ride back through Durotar on a new mount is the class's quiet victory lap. 90+ gold makes it painless — sell banked materials first.
- [ ] Ride into Riverglades and run the full quest arc. The zone is dense, tuned for both factions, and designed for exactly this level band.
- [ ] Group for Krol'dok Stronghold (40–45) when the arc points there. Quest-load, clear once, keep the gear. As the tank, Sunder stacks are the party's damage buff; Demoralizing Shout every pack.
- [ ] Check The Drowned City (35–40) only as an early-arrival fallback; same one-clear rule.
- [ ] Spend deliberately at 40–45: weapon, bags, consumables. Warriors eat sharpening stones and elixirs, and the Well Fed buff never stops paying.

**Gear check:** best 2H owned — at 40 the gap between a fresh weapon and a stale one is the gap between fun and chores. Tank set assembled for Krol'dok: shield, 1H, stamina-heavy pieces.

**What this act teaches:** Mortal Strike plus Sweeping Strikes is the duo's elite deletion service: mark two targets, pop Sweeping, Mortal the skull, Cleave, watch both health bars vanish. Riverglades hands out pairs and trios of mobs constantly — exactly what the kit wants.

## Act 10 — The 50s and the Ceiling, Levels 45–60

The home stretch: the Plaguelands, Un'Goro, the Blasted Lands, and Forever's new dungeon ladder. At 60 the Warrior becomes the most wanted class in endgame: every raid needs tanks, and Fury and Arms both bring competitive damage.

**Goal:** level 60, a spec chosen on evidence, raid-ready by the December 9 raid open.

- [ ] Run the 45–53 band in Felwood, Tanaris, the Hinterlands, or Azshara. Alcaz Prison (48–53) is the dungeon check: quest-load, clear once, tank it.
- [ ] Run the 53–58 band in Un'Goro, the Blasted Lands, or Winterspring. Blackmaw Hold (55–60) covers the late-50s slot.
- [ ] Finish with Shaper's Terrace (58–60), the pre-endgame tuning run. Its quest-loaded clear bridges gear into raid content.
- [ ] Complete the Arms tree: Cruelty 5/5, Unbridled Wrath 5/5, Enrage 5/5, Piercing Howl, Reckless Swings, Lingering Rage 2/2 by 60. If a Protection respec into Fury around 40 was taken as the guides suggest, adapt — the destination is a spec chosen, not inherited.
- [ ] At 60, decide on evidence. Arms for two-hander burst and PvP. Fury for sustained dual-wield damage. Protection if tanking raids is the plan — which for a troll Warrior it probably should be: Berserking does not help the raid, but tanking does.
- [ ] Calendar check: Barrow Deeps, Hyjal Summit, and Onyxia's Lair open December 9. Level 60 in November makes for week-one raid material. Guild up before the ding.

**Gear check:** best weapon obtainable pre-raid — the item brought to Barrow Deeps. Tank and DPS sets both banked, consumables stocked, epic-riding fund started. Warriors are the most gear-hungry class at 60; start the stockpile at 55.

**What this act teaches:** the 50s are where the class's identity catches up with the journey: dozens of dungeons tanked, hundreds of elites Mortal Struck, every bad pull Berserked through since level 4. Arrive at 60 with a spec chosen on purpose, a guild waiting, and a tanking resume nobody else in the raid can match.

## The Handoff: Reading the Character at 60

Before walking into Barrow Deeps, audit the character the road produced.

- [ ] **Spec:** a finished plan, points placed with intent. Arms 32/19/0 or a Protection/Fury route that can be defended.
- [ ] **Weapon skills:** every melee type trained and near cap. A dropped weapon that cannot be used is the slowest kind of loss.
- [ ] **Stances:** Battle, Defensive, Berserker all keybound. The stance dance is the class identity, not a talent.
- [ ] **Threat kit:** Taunt, Sunder Armor, Demoralizing Shout, Battle Shout all ranked and reflexive.
- [ ] **Survival kit:** Victory Rush discipline, Shield Wall, Last Stand if Protection, the Intimidating Shout plus bandage combo rehearsed.
- [ ] **Interrupts:** Pummel ranked; Shield Bash on the tank bar. Casters are no longer kryptonite.
- [ ] **First Aid maxed.** Mandatory at level 4, mandatory at 60.
- [ ] **Consumables:** Well Fed always, sharpening stones, healing potions, elixirs. Warrior damage is a supply chain.
- [ ] **Money:** repair and consume reserves. Warriors spend like sailors.
- [ ] **Guild:** a raid home. Every raid wants Warriors; tanks choose their guild, not the other way around.

If all ten pass, the troll who crawled out of the Valley of Trials with no rage and no buttons is now the reason forty other people get loot.

## Warrior Leveling Systems

Forever's Warrior keeps the rage fantasy and rebuilds the economy under it.

### Rage, Normalized

All weapons generate rage equally over time. Weapon speed no longer matters for rage generation; critical strikes generate double. The biggest quality-of-life change in the class deep dive: slow two-handers are purely a damage choice now, never a rage penalty.

- Rage from crits rewards crit stacking. Agility, crit enchants, and camp-chair crit buffs are rage economy, not just damage.
- Pool before hard fights. Kill the weak mob next to the elite first, walk in with 60+ rage, and the fight is half won. Empty-rage Warriors lose fights they should win.

### Victory Rush

Baseline in Forever. Modest damage plus a 10% heal. Priority: on cooldown whenever under 90% health.

The discipline: heal with kills, not meals. Victory Rush plus bandages covers most recovery. Eating is for dungeon breaks, not every other pull.

### The Ability Economy

- **Rend** is the workhorse. Best damage per rage for 20+ levels. Anything living 9 seconds gets one.
- **Demoralizing Shout** is defense disguised as offense. Melee packs hit noticeably less. Every dungeon pack, every hard mob. Skip versus casters.
- **Thunder Clap early, Whirlwind later.** Thunder Clap is the early AoE; Whirlwind in Berserker Stance takes over for packs.
- **Overpower after every dodge.** 5-second window, tiny rage cost, huge efficiency. Improved Overpower makes it crit.
- **Execute only to finish.** Dumping excess rage into it is a trap; 15 rage, kill shot, done. Nothing worse than killing at 40 rage and facing the next mob empty.
- **Heroic Strike and Cleave are dumps.** 60+ or 85+ rage only. They replace the auto-attack, so they are efficiency losses at low rage.

### Stances

- **Battle Stance:** home. Overpower lives here; most of leveling happens here.
- **Defensive Stance:** dungeons and oh-no moments. Taunt, Sunder, survival.
- **Berserker Stance:** from 30. Whirlwind, Intercept, Pummel, Berserker Rage. The aggressive toolkit.

The dance: spend rage above 10 before swapping stances; stance swaps drop rage hard. Jumping increases Whirlwind and Thunder Clap range — free damage.

### Sunder Armor, Reworked

Sunder now works over a stronger Expose Armor, and its threat scales with Attack Power. Five stacks on a dungeon boss is up to a 30% physical damage party buff. As a tank this is the identity: not just surviving — multiplying the party.

### Stats and Gear

1. **Spirit first while solo.** Forever Warriors regenerate health from Spirit more than any class. It is the heal spell between pulls, and it multiplies with troll Regeneration.
2. **Strength second.** 2 attack power per point. Direct, honest damage.
3. **Agility third.** Crit (which doubles rage generation), dodge, armor.
4. **Stamina lower than instinct says.** Solo, if 1v1 deaths are not happening, Spirit keeps the character safer than a fatter health pool. In dungeons, flip the priority: survival is the tank's job.
5. **Weapon damage per swing is king for Arms.** Swords, then axes and polearms, then maces and staves; slower is always better. Weapon upgrades on schedule are the class's second talent tree.

### Troll Racials on a Warrior

- **Berserking:** 15% attack speed for 10 seconds, 2-minute cooldown. Rage generation on demand. Macro it with burst windows and press it in every real fight.
- **Beast Slaying:** 5% damage against beasts. Most of the leveling diet, plus plenty of raid trash.
- **Regeneration:** passive health regen that keeps ticking. With Forever's Spirit scaling, trolls out-regenerate other Warriors noticeably between pulls.

The honest comparison: orcs bring Blood Fury and Hardiness, undead bring sustain and damage, tauren bring tanking stats. Trolls win leveling outright; endgame damage is close enough that playing well dwarfs the racial gap.

### Professions

- **First Aid:** mandatory. The single most important profession for a Warrior. Level it without mercy.
- **Campfire economy:** Cooking's Basic Campfire Kit anchors every fire; add a fixture for the buff. For pure XP per hour, Leatherworking's Camp Tent is the winner; Blacksmithing's Sharpening Wheel fits Warriors directly. Get one profession to 20 and feed every fire passed.
- **Primary pairings:** Mining + Blacksmithing for crafted gear (sharpening stones are Warrior fuel); Herbalism + Alchemy for self-sufficiency. Both slow leveling slightly. Skinning is strictly worse for Warriors than Blacksmithing.
- **Secondaries:** Cooking for Well Fed XP and campfire kits; Fishing feeds it.

### Money and Mounts

Nobody hands a Warrior a free mount. The raptor at 40 is the biggest purchase of the run, and trolls buy in Sen'jin Village. Bank silver from the Barrens onward, sell every grey, and treat weapon purchases as the only mandatory spending. Banked properly, 40 is painless; if not, sell the cloth and ore stock before complaining.

### Dungeon Roles

Forever Warriors are the tank class: every 5-man wants one, every raid needs several. Taunt the loose, Sunder the boss, Demoralizing Shout every pack, and let the damage dealers work. DPS Warriors are wanted too — especially with Sweeping Strikes cleave value — but the tank slot is where Warriors set the pace of the entire game.

## Speed Rules for Forever

- [ ] **One enemy at a time until Sweeping Strikes.** Warriors lose to pairs until 30. Discipline first, cleave second.
- [ ] **Never fight at half health.** Eat, bandage, Victory Rush. A full health bar is rage banked.
- [ ] **Pool rage before hard fights.** Weak mob first, elite second, victory assured.
- [ ] **Dungeons once, quest-loaded.** Trash XP was gutted; dungeon quests carry the value. One clear per dungeon — tanked.
- [ ] **Campfire every session.** Cooking's kit plus a profession fixture; sit, buff, go. Leatherworking's Camp Tent is the XP king.
- [ ] **Well Fed always.** 5% kill XP from cooked food is the strongest leveling consumable in the game.
- [ ] **Weapon upgrades on schedule.** Auction or quest 2H near 18, 30–32, 40, and the 50s. Weapon damage is the damage.
- [ ] **Berserking on cooldown.** Every real fight, every time it is up. Free haste is free rage.
- [ ] **Line-of-sight casters.** Pull them around corners, let them walk over. Pre-38 Warriors have no interrupt; geometry is the Pummel.
- [ ] **Escape routes exist.** Charge a neutral mob to build distance, Intimidating Shout plus bandage, plan the run before the pull. Dying costs five minutes; escaping costs five seconds.

## Final Word

Warrior leveling is a contract: the worst first ten levels in the game, then the strongest payoff curve of any class. Forever honored both halves — rage normalization softens the opening, Victory Rush feeds the middle, and the new talent tree gives the 30s and 40s real toys. Troll is the best race for the job. Duo with a hunter and the road is faster than either class deserves.
