# New to MMOs? Start Here

This page explains what kind of game WoW: Forever is, assuming no prior MMO experience. It applies to every race and class. Read it once, then move to the [Horde New Player Guide](/docs/new-player/horde) or the guide for your class.

## What an MMO Is

WoW: Forever is a massively multiplayer online game. Thousands of real people share one persistent world.

- **Other players are real.** The character running past is another human. Grouping, trading, and ignoring are all valid choices.
- **The world does not pause.** There is no pause button. Leaving the keyboard in a dangerous area leaves the character exposed.
- **Progress is permanent.** Characters, levels, money, and items stay saved on the game's servers.
- **There is no fail state or timer.** Finishing a level band in one night or one month is equally valid.
- **Late content needs groups.** Dungeons take 5 players; raids take 10, 20, or 40. Learning to play with strangers is a core skill.

## The Core Loop

The game is a loop:

1. Take a quest from a character with a **yellow exclamation mark**.
2. Open the map (**M**) and travel to the objective.
3. Do the thing: kill something, collect something, deliver something.
4. Return to the **yellow question mark** and turn in.
5. Receive experience points (XP), money, or items. Enough XP produces a level.

New zones, enemies, abilities, and people keep the loop changing. The first twenty levels exist to make this loop feel automatic.

## The Character, Explained Simply

| Element | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Health** | The life bar. At zero, the character dies. Refills slowly alone, faster with food or healing. |
| **Resource** | Pays for abilities: Mana, Energy, Rage, or Focus depending on class. When empty, abilities wait. |
| **Experience bar** | Progress toward the next level. Sits near the bottom of the screen. |
| **Bags** | Hold everything picked up. They fill fast; sell junk to vendors often. |
| **Action bar** | The toolkit. Abilities sit here for clicking. Later, **keybinds** move them to keyboard keys. |

## Combat Basics

Combat starts by clicking an enemy to target it, then pressing an attack. Basic attacks fire automatically in range; abilities do the special work.

Three ideas make combat click:

1. **Enemies fight back.** More than one enemy at once is how new players die.
2. **Health refills after combat.** Finishing a fight healthy matters more than finishing fast.
3. **Running away is a real strategy.** Most enemies give up after a short chase. Dying costs time, not progress — but it is still time.

## Pulling: The Most Important Word in the Game

**Pulling** means getting exactly one enemy's attention and bringing that one enemy to a fighting position on purpose, while its friends stand unaware. Hit it from range (a shot, a spell, anything) and let it walk over.

Enemies stand in packs. Running into the middle of a pack means three or four attackers at once, and a new player usually dies. Pulling one at a time means winning every fight.

:::note The one rule
Attack from range, let the enemy come, fight it alone. If a second one comes, run.
:::

Later, in dungeons and raids, pulling becomes a job: someone brings enemies to the group in a controlled way so fights start on the group's terms. Some classes — Hunters especially — inherit that job at high level.

## Targeting

Targeting is clicking (or pressing **Tab**) to choose which enemy receives attacks. Abilities hit whatever is targeted.

1. Click the target before pressing abilities. Attacks with no target do nothing.
2. Check the surroundings before attacking. One wrong click can pull three enemies instead of one.

## Mob Name Colors

A **mob** is any enemy creature controlled by the game.

| Name color | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Yellow** | Neutral. Ignores the character unless attacked first. Safe to walk past. |
| **Red** | Hostile. Attacks on proximity. Plan a path around them or pull them one at a time. |

## Death: What It Actually Costs

Death is a time tax, not a punishment:

1. The character appears at a graveyard as a spirit.
2. Run back to the body and resurrect. Gear takes a small durability hit that costs a little money to repair.

That is the whole cost. No lost levels, items, or quests. The alternative — the **spirit healer** — resurrects instantly but applies weakness for ten minutes plus a bigger repair bill. Corpse runs are almost always correct.

Treat every death as information: too many enemies pulled, wandered too far, or stood in the wrong spot. Then adjust.

## Playing With Other People

Almost the entire game works solo, but grouping is often faster and some content requires it.

- **Invites.** Right-click a player's portrait or name and choose Invite. Accepting invites from players on the same quests is usually worth it.
- **Scaling.** Enemies grow stronger with more players in the group, but the group also kills faster and survives more.
- **Shared credit.** Kill quests normally credit the whole group for each kill anyone makes.
- **Loot rolls.** Good items drop with dice rolls: **Need** if the item will be equipped, **Greed** if it is only for selling. Full rules live in the [Glossary](/docs/reference/glossary).
- **Friends list.** Add friends day one (press **O**, then Add Friend) to see when they are online.

## Chat Basics

Press **Enter**, type, press **Enter** to send. The slash command decides who sees it.

| Command | Audience |
| --- | --- |
| `/s` | Say — people nearby |
| `/y` | Yell — people farther away |
| `/p` | Party — everyone in the group |
| `/g` | Guild — everyone in the guild |
| `/w name` | Whisper — private message |
| `/1`, `/2` | Zone and trade channels |

Two rules carry far: roll Need only on items the character will actually use, and be the player worth grouping with — greet, ask before inviting, thank after.

## First-Session Checklist

- [ ] Log in, create the character, and take ten seconds to find the health bar, resource bar, action bar, and minimap.
- [ ] Accept the first quest (yellow exclamation mark).
- [ ] Open the map (**M**), find the objective, and walk there fighting enemies one at a time.
- [ ] Turn in the quest (yellow question mark) and watch the experience bar move.
- [ ] Sell grey items to a vendor; buy bags if sold.
- [ ] Add friends to the friends list (**O**).
- [ ] Read the zone general channel (`/1`) for a while to see how people talk.
- [ ] If invited to a group for the same quests, accept — and watch how the others pull and fight.
- [ ] Log out somewhere safe, like an inn or town.

## Words You Will Hear

Players say *mob*, *pull*, *aggro*, *tank*, *healer*, *DPS*, *kite*, *BoP*, and a dozen more in the first weeks. Every term is defined in the [Glossary](/docs/reference/glossary). Keep it open in a second window while playing.
