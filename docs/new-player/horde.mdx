# Horde New Player Guide

This guide is for anyone starting a Horde character in World of Warcraft: Forever, regardless of race or class. You do not need previous MMO experience.

The goal for your first session is not to level quickly. It is to learn the basic loop: take a quest, complete it safely, return for a reward, and prepare for the next adventure.

:::tip No rush
Azeroth is not going anywhere. Explore, read ability tooltips, and log off before the game stops being fun.
:::

## Before You Create a Character

World of Warcraft: Forever is included with an active World of Warcraft subscription or Game Time.

1. Create or sign in to a Battle.net account.
2. Install World of Warcraft through the Battle.net desktop app.
3. Select **World of Warcraft: Forever** from the available game versions.
4. If you are joining friends, agree on **Horde** and the same **Ruleset** before anyone creates a character.

Players can group, join guilds, run dungeons, and raid together only when their faction and Ruleset are compatible. These early choices decide who you can play with, so coordinate them first.

## Choose Your Experience Preset

On your first login, Forever asks you to choose an experience preset:

- **Enhanced** enables modern presentation and interface assistance, including quest map guidance and the centralized interface layout.
- **Classic** uses a more traditional presentation and leaves more interface guidance disabled.

If you are unsure, choose **Enhanced**. It is a starting point, not a permanent commitment. You can change individual settings later under **Game Menu > Options**.

## Choose Your Ruleset

Rulesets determine the kind of world and player combat you sign up for:

- **Normal:** Player-versus-player combat is generally at your discretion. This is the best default while learning.
- **PvP:** Opposing players can attack you through much of the open world.
- **Roleplay:** Community naming and behavior rules support playing in character.
- **Roleplay-PvP:** Combines roleplay expectations with frequent open-world PvP risk.

The examples in this guide assume **Normal**. Hardcore is a separate planned experience, not part of this beginner path.

## Create Your Horde Character

Your choices answer different questions:

- **Faction:** Your communities, cities, allies, and many stories. This guide uses **Horde**.
- **Race:** Appearance, starting location, and racial abilities.
- **Class:** Your abilities, combat resource, equipment, and group role.

Read the character-creation descriptions and pick something that appeals to you. You can learn any class; you do not need to choose whatever a tier list currently favors.

High Definition and Standard Definition character models change appearance, not power or progression. Forever also uses a main name and secondary name; the complete combination must be unique in your region. You can hide your own secondary name from your nameplate in the options, though it still appears in chat.

## Learn the Screen

Take a minute to find these elements before fighting anything:

- **Health bar:** When this reaches zero, your character dies.
- **Resource bar:** This pays for class abilities. Its name and behavior depend on your class.
- **Action bar:** Your usable abilities. Put frequently used abilities in comfortable positions.
- **Quest tracker:** Shows objectives for quests you are following.
- **Minimap:** Shows nearby objectives, locations, and points of interest.
- **Bags:** Everything you loot or purchase needs space here.

These default keyboard shortcuts open the main interfaces:

| Key | Opens |
| --- | --- |
| **L** | Quest Log |
| **M** | World Map |
| **P** | Spellbook and abilities |
| **C** | Character panel and equipped gear |
| **K** | Professions |

Press **Escape**, then open **Options**, to adjust controls, action bars, nameplates, audio, display, and accessibility settings. Start with the defaults and change one thing at a time.

## Your First Quest

Look for an NPC with an exclamation point overhead. Talk to them and accept the quest.

1. Read the objective and check the quest tracker.
2. Press **M** to find the objective marker or highlighted area.
3. Travel there, watching enemy levels as you leave familiar roads.
4. Complete the objective: defeating enemies, collecting items, interacting with something, or delivering a message.
5. Return to an NPC with a question mark overhead to turn in the completed quest.
6. Review the reward and equip it if it is appropriate for your class.

If the tracker becomes crowded, open the Quest Log with **L** and untrack quests you are not currently doing. You can also abandon a quest there if you need space.

## Combat: One Enemy at a Time

Target an enemy, start with an ability or attack that fits your class, and watch what the enemy does. Your action bar and spellbook tell you what your abilities do; press **P** if an ability is missing from its expected slot.

The safest beginner habit is to **pull one enemy at a time**. Look around first: nearby enemies, patrols, or a second opponent can turn an easy fight into a corpse run.

Let automatic attacks do their work. You do not need to spend every ability the moment it becomes available. Using everything constantly can empty your class resource and leave you waiting between fights.

Carry suitable food or drink when your class can use it, and visit your class trainer as you level to learn new abilities and ranks.

## Death and Recovery

Death costs time; it does not erase your character, quests, or items.

After releasing, you appear as a spirit near a graveyard. Follow the corpse marker on your map or minimap, approach your body, and resurrect in a safe spot. Do not revive directly beside the enemies that just defeated you.

A Spirit Healer can return you to life at the graveyard when reaching your body is impractical. That convenience can damage equipment durability and apply Resurrection Sickness, which temporarily makes you much weaker. Use the fallback when needed, then recover and repair rather than immediately rejoining combat.

## Bags, Vendors, and Money

Loot fills bags quickly. Visit vendors regularly and sell unwanted items. Gray-quality items are generally safe vendor trash; if another item matters, read its tooltip before selling it.

- Right-click an item while a vendor window is open to sell it.
- Check the vendor's **Buyback** tab if you sell something by mistake.
- Vendors marked with an anvil can repair damaged equipment for a fee.
- Compare quest gear with what you have equipped; higher quality does not automatically mean useful for your class.
- Save coin for training, bags, supplies, and travel rather than buying every attractive item you see.

A small amount of preparation at each town prevents a full bag or broken weapon from ending your next trip early.

## Travel and Rested Time

Flight masters teach flight paths that make later journeys much easier. Click a flight master when you reach a new hub so that destination becomes available.

Innkeepers can bind your Hearthstone to their inn. Binding gives you a repeatable way home, so choose a hub that is useful for your current quests rather than changing it without a reason.

Roads are usually easier to follow than cutting through unfamiliar territory. They are not a guarantee of safety, so still watch nearby enemies.

Log out in an inn or city to accumulate rested experience. This gives you a bonus as you defeat enemies during later play. It is a reward for stopping, not something you need to optimize.

## Professions and Campsites

Professions let you gather materials, craft supplies, and contribute useful objects at shared campsites.

Each character can learn **two primary professions** and all **three secondary professions**:

- Gathering professions include Herbalism, Mining, and Skinning.
- Crafting professions let you create equipment, enhancements, gadgets, potions, and other supplies.
- Cooking, Fishing, and First Aid provide food, ingredients, and survival supplies.

Press **K** to review learned professions, skill levels, recipes, and required materials. Trainers in starting areas and major cities teach professions and later ranks. City guards can point you toward a trainer.

When you see players gathered at a campsite, stop and investigate. Campsites support resting, trading, and sharing profession-made benefits. Contribute what your professions can provide, and take advantage of the shared preparation before heading out.

## Playing with Other People

Most early content can be learned alone, but grouping makes difficult objectives easier and introduces you to other players.

A typical dungeon group has five players:

- A **tank** controls enemy attention and sets the approach.
- A **healer** keeps the group alive.
- **Damage dealers (DPS)** defeat enemies while handling their assigned targets and mechanics.

Follow the group's plan, communicate before rushing ahead, and ask when you do not understand something. If equipment drops, ask before rolling **Need**; use it for something you will genuinely equip and use, rather than merely something you want to sell.

Guilds are social groups that can help with questions, grouping, professions, and later activities. Different guilds have different expectations and communication channels, so read their onboarding and ask where questions belong. You do not need to join the first guild invitation you receive.

Be patient with other beginners. Everyone in Azeroth once had a first quest and a first corpse run.

## First-Session Checklist

Use this as your definition of a successful first session:

- [ ] Coordinate Horde and Ruleset with any friends before creating characters.
- [ ] Choose Enhanced if you want modern interface guidance.
- [ ] Create a race and class you want to learn.
- [ ] Find your health, resource bar, action bar, minimap, and bags.
- [ ] Accept a quest and track its objective.
- [ ] Defeat enemies one at a time and loot them.
- [ ] Turn in the quest and review the reward.
- [ ] Sell unwanted gray items and check vendor Buyback if needed.
- [ ] Visit useful trainers and learn a flight point when you reach a hub.
- [ ] Bind your Hearthstone at a convenient inn.
- [ ] Log out safely in an inn or city.

You do not need to finish a particular level or zone. If you understand the quest loop and can prepare for another session, the guide has done its job.
