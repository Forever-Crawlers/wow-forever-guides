# Frequently Asked Questions

Distilled from a launch-week Q&A session covering the orc hunter and troll warrior paths. Items marked **beta-reported** come from pre-launch data or launch-week reports and should be verified in game before being relied on.

## Game Structure

### Is there a main quest?

No. Forever uses hubs, chains, and breadcrumbs. No single golden path — pick a hub, finish its chains, follow a breadcrumb when offered, check the zone band table in [Leveling](/docs/leveling) when quests run dry. The only structural "main" content is the raid attunement chains at 60.

### Which races can level together from level 1?

| Friend's race | Starts in | When you can quest together |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Troll (with an orc) | Durotar — Valley of Trials | Immediately, level 1 |
| Tauren | Mulgore | Around 12–13, the Barrens |
| Undead | Tirisfal Glades | Around 13, Barrens or Orgrimmar (zeppelin) |

Do not cross the map early to meet a tauren or undead friend — the trip eats an hour. Level separately, meet at the Crossroads. Kill quests credit both members of a party, and both can loot and skin the same beasts.

### Is troll warrior a good combo?

One of the best. Berserking is the strongest warrior racial — attack speed converts directly to Rage, so it is a resource button. Beast Slaying works against nearly everything killed while leveling. Regeneration is small, passive, free. Launch-week class guides rate troll the best Horde warrior race for leveling; tauren has a slight edge only for dedicated endgame tanking. Pairing with a hunter is strong: the pet tanks while the warrior builds Rage.

## Professions

### Can professions be changed?

Yes, at any time, free and instant. Open the spellbook, find the profession, and press **Unlearn**. All skill and recipes are lost permanently. There is no cost and no cooldown.

### Which professions should a hunter take?

**Skinning immediately, Leatherworking at level 10–12.** Skinning turns every beast killed into gold while leveling. Leatherworking pairs with it for free gear and bags through the 40s. Alternative for pure income: Skinning + Mining, selling everything.

### Which professions should a warrior take?

**Mining + Blacksmithing.** Sharpening stones are warrior fuel for 60 levels; Blacksmithing makes them, Mining feeds the ore for free. If a leveling partner already has Leatherworking, Blacksmithing is doubly correct — the pair covers both campfire fixtures (see below).

### What about secondary professions?

Not optional for either class: **First Aid** is the warrior's main heal until the 40s and stays the panic button at 60; **Cooking** produces Well Fed food (+5% kill XP, the strongest leveling consumable) and the Basic Campfire Kit every fixture requires; **Fishing** feeds Cooking. Take Fishing when a trainer is convenient.

### Campfire fixtures — what are they?

At profession **skill 20**, each primary unlocks a campfire fixture with a buff that scales with character level: Leatherworking's Camp Tent (+XP), Blacksmithing's Sharpening Wheel, Cooking's Basic Campfire Kit. The buff scales off character level, not profession skill — a level-20 Mining skill on a level-30 character still grants a level-30 buff. Level both primaries to 20 as early as possible; it is the cheapest power in the game.

### How does Skinning work?

Kill the beast, **loot the corpse first**, then click the skinning icon on the cleaned corpse. Requires a Skinning Knife **in the bags** (passive, like the Mining Pick — no equipping). Skinnable corpses belonging to other players are usable once they finish looting. One quirk that may or may not survive patches: the Basic Campfire buff applies a tiny crit buff to the next skin.

### Is Leatherworking worth keeping with Skinning?

Yes. Crafted leather pieces fill empty slots through the 40s, and the Kamari level-10 quest (see below) grants a profession bag. It is not an endgame profession, but it pays for itself long before then.

## Economy

### What should be kept versus sold?

**Keep:** leather and hides for Leatherworking; 2–3 stacks of each cloth type for First Aid and tailoring friends; pet food matching the pet's diet (meat and fish for cats); green items until auctioned. **Sell:** everything grey; "Ruined..." scraps; BoP quest rewards that are upgrades over nothing; greens that cannot be used once the auction house is unlocked. Money is the level-40 mount fund.

### Do gathering tools need bag slots or equipping?

Passive in bags: Skinning Knife, Mining Pick, Blacksmith Hammer. Must be equipped: Fishing Pole.

### What should be bought at the auction house around level 12?

**Hunter:** two 8–10 slot bags (first priority), a better ranged weapon if still white (few silver, not gold), trivially cheap ammunition upgrades. Skip armor (quest gear carries to 20), small bags, pet items, recipes, consumables. **Warrior:** two 8–10 slot bags, then a slow two-handed green weapon around level 10–15 (Rough price cap: a few silver), plus Rough Grinding Stones if none are crafted yet.

Price discipline: walk in with a hard budget equal to what greys and leather just sold for. Combine the trip with weapon-master training, thread from the LW supply vendor, and bulk pet food.

### Where does Cured Light Hide come from? (Beasts of Thunder Ridge)

Not a drop — it is a Leatherworking craft: 1 Light Hide + 1 Salt (a few copper from the LW supply vendor). The quest (from Kamari, Orgrimmar, level 10) also wants 5 Rough Lizard Hides from the thunder lizards in Thunder Ridge, the ravine northwest of Razor Hill (~39, 28). Reward: **Pattern: Light Leather Reagent Bag** — a profession bag that frees normal bag slots. For a skinner/leatherworker this quest is part of the profession path, not a side errand.

### What about quests nobody has data for? ("Where is X?")

Launch-week rule, in order:

1. Read the quest log text — it usually names the holder or the camp.
2. Check the zone map; Forever marks objectives, named targets get markers.
3. For random drops, stay in the named camp until it drops.
4. Search the Wowhead Forever database by quest name.
5. Ask general chat — at launch, half the server is on the same quest.

## Hunter

### What skills matter at level 10?

From the trainer: **Serpent Sting, Arcane Shot, Track Beasts**. Then the taming chain in order: Taming the Beast (tame a Dire Mottled Boar) → Taming the Beast (tame a Surf Crawler along the coast) → Taming the Beast (tame an Armored Scorpid — abandon after) → complete to unlock Tame Beast, Call Pet, Dismiss Pet, Feed Pet, Revive Pet, and a pet slot. Finish the chain before judging any "missing spell."

### What is the rotation at 10–12?

1. Send the pet in — the pet tanks, the hunter never does.
2. One Serpent Sting per mob.
3. Arcane Shot on spare Focus.
4. Auto Shot fires by itself — no button needed.
5. Concussive Shot only if the mob turns toward the hunter.

### Which pet, and is there a better one at 12?

Keep the cat. All pet families deal the same baseline damage in Forever; families differ in abilities and durability. Cats bring Claw plus high attack speed and eat the two most common food types. Real upgrades are **ability ranks**: tame wild beasts carrying higher Bite ranks, learn the rank, release the teacher, teach the cat. At ~16, a Barrens quest (Sergra Darkthorn) summons the white lion Echeyakee — tameable during the summon; a style upgrade, not a power spike. Swap to a boar only if casters at range are a persistent problem (Boar Charge fixes pulling them).

### What happens when the pet dies?

Three cases, in order of severity:

1. **Pet dies, hunter lives:** wait until combat ends, then Revive Pet.
2. **Pet despawns mid-fight:** it is not dead. Cast Call Pet.
3. **Both die:** the pet resurrects with the hunter — at zero happiness. Feed immediately or it deserts (runs away permanently).

Death drains happiness. Feed after every death, not just when the icon turns yellow.

### How is the pet fed — and why did the feed macro break?

Manual: open the spellbook, click Feed Pet, then click the food in the bags. Macro (preferred): `/cast [@pet] Feed Pet` + `/use <exact food name>`. Never use `/use 0 1` — if a weapon or fishing pole occupies the first bag slot, the macro equips it instead of feeding. Known launch-week bug: Feed Pet or Revive Pet sometimes missing from the spellbook — finish the taming chain, relog, check the trainer, then file a bug report. Do not release the pet.

### Which hunter talents?

The Beast Mastery highway, taken the moment each tier opens:

| Level | Talent | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | Improved Aspect of the Monkey 5/5 | +8% dodge while Monkey is up |
| 15 | Thick Hide 3/3 | Pet armor and own armor +30% |
| 20 | Improved Revive Pet 2/2 | Faster, cheaper revive |
| 25 | Pathfinding 2/2 | +8% outdoor speed, both aspects |
| 30 | Bestial Discipline 2/2 | Pet Focus regen +100% |
| 35 | Unleashed Fury 5/5 | +20% pet damage |
| 40 | Frenzy 5/5 → Bestial Wrath | Pet crits grant attack speed; the wrath window every 2 min |

Nothing else is close for pure leveling speed. Frenzy before Bestial Wrath is the correct order — it is the prerequisite and the rage engine.

### What is the level-10 checklist?

Train new skills (Serpent Sting, Arcane Shot, Track Beasts), tame the pet and keep it fed, buy the level-10 bow from the Razor Hill bowyer (~10–15 silver), buy two 6-slot bags plus ammunition (~5–10 silver), keep First Aid trained, set Mend Pet (12) on a comfortable key, two professions running (Skinning + Leatherworking), continue stacking Tough Jerky for food.

### How should levels 13–20 be handled?

Standard path: finish Durotar around 12–13, then the Barrens 13–20, RFC at 13–16, Wailing Caverns at 17–20. The power-leveling variant if a higher-level friend helps: RFC repetitions 13–16 (four clean runs with quests ≈ a level each), then stacked Crossroads circuits 16–20 — kill quests gathered as one bundle, turn in as one loop. The stacking technique: collect every kill quest in a hub, add a taskkill while traveling between camps, so every kill counts double or triple. Circuit, not tunnel.

## Warrior

### What is the rotation at level 8?

Before every fight: eat to full, Battle Shout up. Every fight: 1. **Charge** (always — stun, gap close, opening Rage), 2. **Rend** on anything living 9+ seconds, 3. **Thunder Clap** on one hard mob or two mobs (skip on easy singles), 4. **Heroic Strike** only at 40+ Rage — it replaces the auto-attack, so pressing early is a loss, 5. **Victory Rush** when available and below 90% health. Between fights: bandage; Berserking when it is up.

### What changes at level 10?

Rage starts arriving from swings instead of only Charge, which unlocks the full six-step rotation: Charge → Rend → Thunder Clap → Heroic Strike at 40+ Rage → Victory Rush on kill → repeat. Train every weapon skill at a weapon master, not just the racial favorites. Weapon choice until the Weaponmaster talent (level 20+): whatever hits hardest per swing. Stances: Battle Stance is default; Defensive Stance (level 10, quest) for tanking or surviving; Berserker Stance (30, quest) for damage — 2H weapon in any stance. Train them all on cooldown and visit the trainer every level through the 30s.

### How does Rage generation work in Forever?

**Beta-reported:** rage is normalized — all weapons generate equally per point of damage dealt, and critical strikes generate double. Misses generate zero. Damage taken also feeds rage. Treat the exact coefficients as approximate until confirmed on live.

### Does Rend stack?

No — refreshing early overwrites the debuff and discards remaining ticks. Let it fall off naturally, or accept the loss only when the mob will die within the new window.

### Which talents, 10–24?

Improved Rend 3/3 (10–12) → Deflection (13–14, 19, 23–24) → Improved Overpower 2/2 (15–16) → Improved Charge 2/2 (17–18) → Deep Wounds 3/3 (20–22). At 30: Sweeping Strikes. At 40: Mortal Strike. Plan through level 60: finish Cruelty, Unbridled Wrath, Enrage, Lingering Rage. Trainer visits every level through the 30s; warriors get something useful almost every level.
